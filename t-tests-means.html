Unit 4 · Topics 4.4–4.10 · t-Tests for Means

t-Tests for Means & Paired Data

Why we use t when σ is unknown, one-sample, matched-pairs, and two-sample t-test formulas, degrees of freedom, and conditions.

1. Why t Instead of z? & Variable Definitions

In real problems, the population standard deviation σ is almost always unknown, so we replace σ with the sample standard deviation s. Because s adds extra variability from sample to sample, the standardized statistic follows a Student's t-distribution with heavier tails than N(0, 1). As df → ∞, t(df) → N(0, 1).

Symbol Name Definition & Notes
x, μ0 Sample Mean & Null Mean x = observed sample mean; μ0 = hypothesized population mean under H0.
s Sample Standard Deviation s = √Σ(xi − x)2n − 1. Plugged in for unknown σ.
df Degrees of Freedom Controls tail thickness of t-curve. df = n − 1 for 1-sample / paired; Welch's df for 2-sample.
di, d, sd Paired Differences di = x1i − x2i for pair i; d = mean of differences; sd = SD of differences; n = number of pairs.
μd True Mean Difference Population mean of paired differences (μd = μ1 − μ2). Null is usually H0: μd = 0.

2. The Three t-Test Formulas

1-Sample t-Test for a Population Mean (H0: μ = μ0)
t = x − μ0 s / √n     with     df = n − 1
Normal / Large Condition: Population is Normal, OR n ≥ 30 (CLT), OR sample data plot shows no strong skewness or outliers (if n < 30).
Matched Pairs t-Test (H0: μd = 0)
t = d − μd,0 sd / √n     with     df = n − 1  (n = number of pairs)
Key Idea: Compute the difference di = After − Before for every pair first, then run a standard 1-sample t-test on the single list of differences!
2-Sample t-Test for Independent Means (H0: μ1 − μ2 = 0)
t = (x1 − x2) − (μ1 − μ2)0 √ s12n1 + s22n2
Degrees of Freedom (Unpooled / Welch's): Use technology's exact Welch df, or conservative df = min(n1 − 1, n2 − 1) by hand. Never pool sample variances for 2-sample t unless explicitly told σ1 = σ2.

3. Choosing Correctly: Paired t vs. 2-Sample t

Question to Ask Matched Pairs t-Test 2-Sample t-Test
Are the two measurements linked? Yes — Same subject measured twice (Before/After), twins, or matched pairs. No — Two completely separate, independent random samples or treatment groups.
Sample Sizes Must have equal counts (n1 = n2 = n pairs). n1 and n2 can be different.
Degrees of Freedom df = n − 1 Welch df (up to n1 + n2 − 2) or min(n1 − 1, n2 − 1)
Normal Condition Checked On The single list of differences di. Both Sample 1 and Sample 2 separately!

4. Calculator Syntax & Common Traps

Common Mistakes
  • Using a 2-sample t-test on paired Before/After data (violates independence between the two groups!).
  • Checking n1 + n2 ≥ 30 instead of checking each sample n1 ≥ 30 AND n2 ≥ 30 separately.
  • Using a z-test when given sample SD s instead of population SD σ.
TI-84 / Nspire Commands
  • p-value from t: tcdf(lower, upper, df)
  • Critical value t*: invT(area_left, df) (e.g., 95% CI → invT(0.975, df))
  • Menus: T-Test (1-sample & paired on diff list), 2-SampTTest (unpooled: Pooled: No).
