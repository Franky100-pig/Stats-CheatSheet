Unit 05 · Topic 01 · Sampling Distributions

Sampling Distributions & Central Limit Theorem

Exact notation, parameter vs. statistic distinctions, standard error formulas, and the conditions required for Normal approximations.

1. Variable Definitions: Parameter vs. Statistic

Never mix up parameters (fixed, usually unknown numbers describing the entire population) and statistics (random variables computed from a sample that vary from sample to sample).

Symbol Name Definition & Role Type
μ Population Mean True arithmetic average of all N individuals in the population. Parameter (fixed)
x Sample Mean Arithmetic average of a sample of size n: x = (1/n)Σxi. Statistic (random)
σ, σ2 Population SD / Variance True spread of individual values in the population around μ. Parameter (fixed)
s, s2 Sample SD / Variance Sample spread around x using n − 1 in denominator (Bessel's correction). Statistic (random)
p Population Proportion True fraction of successes in the population (0 ≤ p ≤ 1). Parameter (fixed)
p Sample Proportion Fraction of successes in a sample: p = X / n (X = count of successes). Statistic (random)
n, N Sample / Population Size n = number of observations in sample; N = total size of population. Counts (n ≥ 1)
μx, σx Mean & SD of x Center and spread of the sampling distribution of all possible sample means of size n. Sampling Dist.
SE Standard Error Estimated standard deviation of a statistic computed by plugging in s or p when σ or p is unknown. Estimated Spread

2. Sampling Distribution of a Sample Mean (x)

Suppose we take all possible Simple Random Samples (SRS) of size n from a population with mean μ and standard deviation σ.

Center, Spread & Standardization of x
μx = μ     |     σx = σ√n     |     SEx = s√n     |     z = x − μ σ / √n
Variables: x = observed sample mean; μ = population mean; σ = population SD; s = sample SD; n = sample size.
Key Insight: To cut the standard deviation of x in half, you must multiply sample size n by 4.
Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

Even if the parent population distribution is skewed or non-Normal, as sample size n increases, the sampling distribution of x becomes approximately Normal: x ~ N(μ, σ / √n). Standard threshold used in intro/AP stats: n ≥ 30 (if the population is already Normal, x is exact Normal for any n ≥ 1).

3. Sampling Distribution of a Sample Proportion (p)

When counting binary successes (X ~ Binomial(n, p)) and converting to a sample proportion p = X / n:

Center, Spread & Standardization of p
μp = p     |     σp = √ p(1 − p)n     |     SEp = √ p(1 − p)n     |     z = p − p √p(1 − p) / n
Variables: p = true population proportion; p = X/n = observed sample proportion; 1 − p (often denoted q) = probability of failure; n = sample size.

4. Two-Sample Sampling Distributions (Differences)

For two independent random samples of sizes n1 and n2, the mean is the difference of centers, while variances ALWAYS add (never subtract variances!).

Statistic Center (Mean) True Standard Deviation (σ) Standard Error (SE)
Difference of Means
x1 − x2		 μ1 − μ2 √ σ12n1 + σ22n2 √ s12n1 + s22n2
Difference of Proportions
p1 − p2		 p1 − p2 √ p1(1 − p1)n1 + p2(1 − p2)n2 √ p1(1 − p1)n1 + p2(1 − p2)n2

5. Required Conditions Checklist (R-I-N)

Condition What It Guarantees How to Check (Means vs. Proportions)
1. Random Unbiasedness: μx = μ or μp = p Data collected from an SRS, stratified/cluster random sample, or randomized experiment.
2. Independent (10% Rule) Validates spread formulas (σ/√n) when sampling without replacement Check n ≤ 0.10N (or N ≥ 10n). Note: Not needed for randomized experiments with random assignment!
3. Normal / Large Sample Normal shape so z-table / normalcdf applies Means: Population is Normal, OR n ≥ 30 (CLT), OR sample plot has no strong skew/outliers.
Proportions (Large Counts): np ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) ≥ 10 (use p or p0 depending on context).

6. Common Traps & Calculator Syntax

Common Mistakes
  • Using σ instead of σ/√n when asked about a sample mean x (read the prompt: one individual vs. average of n individuals!).
  • Subtracting variances: σx1−x2 ≠ √σ12/n1 − σ22/n2. Variances always add.
  • Applying the CLT (n ≥ 30) to proportions. Proportions require the Large Counts check (np ≥ 10, n(1−p) ≥ 10).
Calculator & Exam Quick Check
  • Probability for x: normalcdf(lower, upper, μ, σ/√n)
  • Probability for p: normalcdf(lower, upper, p, √(p(1-p)/n))
  • Unbiasedness: A statistic is unbiased if its sampling distribution mean equals the parameter (E(x) = μ).
  • Precision vs. Accuracy: Larger n lowers spread (higher precision), while random sampling prevents bias (accuracy).
